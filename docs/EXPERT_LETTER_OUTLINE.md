# Expert Letter Outline — Original Contribution of Major Significance

This is a structural outline for an independent expert to write from, not
a script to hand someone verbatim. USCIS adjudicators are trained to spot
letters that all sound alike or that clearly weren't written by the
purported author — the letter-writer should read the project, form their
own view, and use this only as a checklist of what to cover.

## 1. Who the expert is and why their opinion carries weight
- Their credentials, role, years in the field
- What makes them qualified to assess this specific contribution
  (not just "I am an engineer" — specific relevant expertise)
- How they know the petitioner or came to review the work (be honest —
  "reviewed at the petitioner's request" is fine and normal)

## 2. The problem the contribution addresses
- What gap existed before this (e.g., RMD/tax-exposure tools were either
  closed-source commercial products or undocumented spreadsheets with no
  visible methodology)
- Why that gap mattered to the field, not just to one company

## 3. What was specifically built
- Plain description of the technical contribution: an open-source,
  methodology-transparent RMD projection engine with an AI narration
  layer, sourced directly from IRS Uniform Lifetime Table data
- What is technically non-obvious or non-trivial about it (the expert's
  own assessment — e.g., separating deterministic calculation from AI
  narration so results stay auditable is a deliberate, defensible design
  choice, not a commodity pattern)

## 4. Evidence of significance and independent adoption
This is the section USCIS weighs most heavily — it needs concrete,
checkable facts, not adjectives:
- GitHub stars / forks / issues from unaffiliated users
- Specific individuals or organizations (other than the petitioner) who
  have adopted, forked, or cited it
- Any press, blog coverage, HN/Reddit discussion threads
- Downloads if published as an npm package

*(Note: gather this evidence before requesting the letter — a letter
that says "this appears significant" without adoption data is much
weaker than one that can point to specific numbers.)*

## 5. How it compares to the existing state of the art
- Brief, honest comparison to closed-source competitors or existing
  spreadsheet-based approaches
- Why openness and methodology transparency is itself a meaningful
  contribution to how the field approaches this problem

## 6. The expert's overall assessment
- A direct statement, in the expert's own words, of whether this rises
  to the level of a significant contribution to the field
- Willingness to be contacted by USCIS to verify the letter if needed

## What to avoid
- Generic superlatives with no specifics ("groundbreaking," "revolutionary")
  unless backed by the evidence in section 4
- Letters that read like a resume summary of the petitioner rather than
  an assessment of the specific contribution
- Any claim the expert can't personally verify or defend if questioned
